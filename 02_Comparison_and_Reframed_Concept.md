## 2. Is the gaming idea acceptable as written?

Not as written. It is five modules (Home/Market/Squad/Arena/Community), the biggest module (hardware rental) targets the smallest part of the audience, and its tournament-fee revenue sits inside a regulated area. A professor would ask "who is the renter?" and "what is actually unique?" and the current documents can't answer either. The fix is to narrow it and borrow the best mechanism from each other idea.

## 3. Reframed concept: "GGX (Good Game Exchange): the Campus Gaming Economy"

**One-line pitch:** a closed-loop campus gaming economy where gaming gear, skill and time are the currencies, and every exchange is verified, QR-tracked and reputation-building.

**Three things borrowed or changed, which together are the unique part:**

1. **Gaming Credits (from SkillSwap).** Students earn credits for contributions: coaching a teammate, organising or casting a match, lending gear, making clips or streams. They spend credits on gear rentals, tournament passes and merch. The brief says 70%+ of Gen Z gamers mostly consume media, so rewarding creators and organizers fits the data better than rewarding wins. Legal guardrail: credits are non-cashable and never tied to winnings or stakes, which keeps us clear of the money-gaming ban.
2. **Event-style commerce for Arena (from Live).** Tournaments and watch parties are sold and managed like events: QR pass wallet, organizer dashboard, sponsor placements. Pilot tournaments are free or sponsor-funded, with fees only for offline passes. This is legally safe and gives the highest transaction frequency.
3. **Hardware narrowed to what the data supports.** Lead with mobile-gaming accessories (triggers, controllers, headsets, power banks, cooling), plus a small shared pool for consoles and PCs. Rental stays, but as a category instead of the whole product. Differentiation from Cashify Play: peer-to-peer, same campus, handover in minutes, no courier, and a reputation score replaces most of the deposit.

**Why this is stronger for the professor:**
- It is a multi-sided platform with a named cold-start strategy (seed via societies, free starter credits).
- It has a trust-and-risk design (QR handover, condition photos, deposit by risk tier) tied to real mobile-commerce flows (UPI, QR, push notifications).
- It reconciles the weaknesses of the other two ideas in the same class folder, and the three research reports can be cited together as a feasibility screen: three ideas assessed, one selected on evidence.
- Every assumption is labelled and gets tested with a primary survey.

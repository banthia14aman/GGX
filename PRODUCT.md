# Product

## Register

brand

## Users
IIT Delhi students who game: mostly mobile players (BGMI, Free Fire), plus a smaller, higher-spending group on PC and console (Valorant, CS2, EA FC). They browse on a phone in a hostel room, often late at night between matches. Secondary audiences are gaming-society organizers, sponsors, and, for this project, an MBA Mobile Commerce professor judging the concept at first glance.

## Product Purpose
GGX (Good Game Exchange) is a verified campus gaming economy: buy, sell and rent gear (consoles, controllers, mobile accessories, physical skins), trade CS2 skins through Steam, find teammates, and enter tournaments with QR passes. Contributions earn non-cashable Gaming Credits. Success for the website: the professor sees a credible, complete, exciting platform in under a minute and believes it could launch on campus.

## Brand Personality
Loud, competitive, trustworthy. It should feel like a live esports broadcast (kill-feed, scoreboards, lower-thirds), with the energy of a match about to start, and still read as a safe place to hand over a ₹10,000 deposit. Voice is short, confident, gamer-native ("GG", "queue up", "loadout"), never cringe or try-hard.

## Anti-references
- Generic AI-made SaaS landing pages: centered hero, gradient text, identical icon cards, hero-metric blocks.
- College notice-board or ERP look.
- Generic RGB "gamer" neon soup (rainbow glows everywhere, Razer/ROG imitation).
- Emojis anywhere on the site.
- Casino or loot-box visuals (case-opening spinners, jackpots): GGX is not gambling.

## Design Principles
1. Broadcast, not brochure: present the marketplace like a live match feed, with real items, real prices and real activity.
2. Show the real thing: actual product and skin imagery, real Indian prices, never placeholder boxes.
3. Trust is part of the hype: the Guarantee, verified IITD identity, held payments and reputation are shown as prominently as the gear.
4. Red means action: the red glow marks what to do next and what is live, never decoration.
5. Earned familiarity in the app screens: standard marketplace patterns, styled with the broadcast vocabulary.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
WCAG 2.1 AA: text contrast at least 4.5:1, visible focus states, 44px touch targets, status never shown by colour alone (CS2 rarity always paired with its name), `prefers-reduced-motion` respected for glows, tickers and reveals. No emojis; icons have accessible labels.
